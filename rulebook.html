Goal

You wake alone in the Entry Hall at 9 PM. Somewhere in the house is the relic that holds the dead here; out behind it is the family plot. Find the relic, bury it there, and be standing when it's done — all before midnight.

There are no dice. Every fight is arithmetic, and the only clock that matters is the development deck running dry.

What's in the box

  • 8 indoor tiles and 8 outdoor tiles, drawn from two separate face-down stacks. Every one of them is laid out on the tiles page.
  • 9 development cards. Each one says a different thing depending on the hour — you read only the current hour's line.
  • Health, Attack and the time are just numbers. The game tracks them for you, beside the board.

Setup

  1. The Entry Hall goes down first. The Veranda is set aside — it only appears when you first step outside.
  2. The remaining indoor and outdoor tiles are shuffled into two separate face-down stacks.
  3. The 9 development cards are shuffled and the top two are burned face down, unseen. That leaves seven live cards per hour.
  4. You start with Attack 1 and Health 6. Both move up and down, and neither has an upper limit.
  5. The clock starts at 9 PM.

You begin in the Entry Hall and draw no card for it — the game only starts once you walk out. Come back to the Entry Hall later, though, and you draw as normal.

Taking a turn

  1. Move. Pick a door out of the room you're in, into either unexplored space or somewhere you've already been. Moving is mandatory — you can't peer into a room and decide to stay put. You can flee after walking into trouble, but you did walk in.
  2. Place the tile if the space is unexplored. The top tile of the stack is revealed and turned for you, under one constraint: one of its doors must line up with the door you just came through. Where there's a choice, it's turned to leave you a way on into unexplored space. Its other doors don't have to match anything — a door can open onto a blank wall, and a wall can seal off a neighbour's door. That's intended. There's no fixed footprint either; the house sprawls as far as the tiles take it.
  3. Draw a development card. Always — even when re-entering a room you already know. Read only the line for the current hour:
    • Item — you may draw the next card and take the item pictured on that one. Declining costs nothing, but you get nothing.
    • Risen — fight them, or run.
    • Event — gain or lose the Health shown, if any.
    If the deck is empty when you need to draw, an hour has passed.
  4. Apply the room's own instructions, after the card is finished. Only some rooms have any.
  5. Check for a dead end. Nowhere left to go means zombie doors.
  6. Kitchen and Herb Garden give +1 Health if you finish your turn in them.
  7. Cower, if you want to. See Cowering.

Special rooms

  • Reliquary. The relic is here. Resolve the room's card as normal, then draw and resolve a second card — that one is the search itself. Survive it, still standing in the room, and the relic is yours. Carrying it does not use an item slot.
  • Family Plot. The same two-card procedure, this time standing for the burial. Survive it holding the relic and you've won.
  • Pantry. Resolve the card as normal, then you may draw one more and take the item on it.
  • Kitchen and Herb Garden. +1 Health if you end your turn there.

Going outside

The only way out of the house is the Dining Hall's exterior door, marked with an arrow. It's one of the Dining Hall's four ordinary doors, not a secret fifth exit.

Take it and the Veranda goes down against the Dining Hall with the two arrows aligned. Then you draw a card as usual.

Outside, turns work exactly as they do indoors with one substitution: instead of a door, you leave across an open grassy edge. Hedges are walls — you can't push through them. As indoors, one grassy edge on each newly placed tile has to line up with the edge you left by.

The clock

Time doesn't pass on a turn counter. It passes when the deck does. Need to draw a card with none left, and an hour has gone by. From that point on you read the new hour's line on every card.

The deck is then reshuffled — including the two burned at setup — and two are burned again.

Hours run 9 PM → 10 PM → 11 PM. There is no midnight line to read; see Losing.

Edge case: if an Item card is the last one in the deck, the deck reshuffles and burns first, then you draw the top of the fresh deck to see what you found.

Combat

Fighting is arithmetic, not dice:

Health lost = (number of risen) − (your Attack)

You can never lose more than 4 Health in a single fight, and you can never gain Health from one.

Attack never stacks. You use exactly one weapon per fight — the best one you're holding — and its bonus doesn't accumulate with anything else. Carrying the Splintered Bone (+1) and the Billhook (+2) leaves you at +2, not +3. Drop a weapon and its bonus goes with it.

Running away

Instead of fighting, you can leave through a door or grassy edge into a room you've already explored. The risen get a parting swipe: −1 Health. You do not draw a card for the room you retreat into.

You can only run one step, to an adjacent explored room — see House rules.

Holding Lamp Oil, you can throw it as you go and take no damage at all. One use.

Cowering

Once a room's turn is finished you may hole up and catch your breath: +3 Health, at the cost of discarding the top development card unresolved. Health is cheap. Time is not.

  • Any tile counts, including outdoor ones.
  • You may cower after running away — the turn resolved, it just didn't happen to involve a card.
  • You may cower between the Reliquary's or Family Plot's two cards. It behaves like an ordinary fresh turn, so cowering there does not use up your end-of-turn one — those rooms give you two chances to steady yourself, one before each card.
  • You may not cower before a zombie-door attack. Afterwards is fine.
  • Once per turn — see House rules.

Zombie doors

Sometimes you end up somewhere with nowhere to go. The classic case is the Washroom placed directly above the Entry Hall, its only door facing back the way you came. It also happens when every exit is explored and the room you still need hasn't turned up.

When that happens, three of the risen smash through a blank wall, making a new opening. Fight them as normal.

Which wall they pick is chosen for you, and favours one facing unexplored space — the hole is how you get out of here, so an opening back into rooms already placed would be no use.

  • They arrive after the room's own card is resolved, not instead of it. Deal with the room first, then with the fact that it's a dead end. A bad tile can therefore mean two fights in one turn, and you can't cower between them.
  • You may run from them. If you do, the new opening is made in the room you end up in, and it stays there.
  • The hole is a hole, not a matched pair of doors — the tile beyond it goes down wall-to-wall, in any rotation. It persists, and you can use it again later.
  • You don't have to go through straight away. Cower first if you like and head through on a later turn. Entering draws a card as normal.

Items

You can carry two at a time. Picking up a third means dropping one, and anything you drop is gone as soon as you leave the room. You may carry two weapons but only ever use one in a fight. The relic is exempt and doesn't count toward the limit.

Winning & losing

You win by surviving the burial of the relic in the Family Plot. Every one of the risen drops where it stands.

You lose if:

  • the risen finish you off,
  • an Event takes your last Health, or
  • you need a development card during the 11 PM hour and the deck is empty. Midnight.

House rules

Three ordinary situations the original rules never settled. These are our calls, and they're what the game above actually does.

GapOur ruling
How far can you flee?One step, to an adjacent explored room
How often can you cower?Once per chance to. An ordinary turn gives one, at the end; the Reliquary and Family Plot give a second between their two cards
Can the Reliquary be re-searched?No — the second-card draw happens once, and is used up on success
